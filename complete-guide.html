The complete ConfigKit user guide

Welcome to ConfigKit. This guide explains how to get started, create and protect an account, find your way around the website, understand the main tools and workspaces, publish and collaborate, and get help when something does not work. It is written for first-time visitors as well as returning members who want one place to understand the full ConfigKit experience.

1. Welcome: getting started as a beginner

ConfigKit is a browser-based collection of tools for configuration profiles, personal organisation, learning, creative projects and community participation. You do not need to understand every feature before using the website: begin at the ConfigKit homepage, read the descriptions of the tools that interest you, and open one workspace at a time. Some pages are public and can be explored without signing in, while features that save personal information, publish content, follow creators, manage files or collaborate require an account and may also depend on permissions or relationships. A good first visit is to look through the Official Wiki, which acts as a directory of the available pages, and then use the Downloads page if you want a desktop package or platform-specific installation instructions. ConfigKit is continuously developed, so the controls and availability of individual features can change; the page itself and its current on-screen instructions should always take priority over an older screenshot or guide.

If you are completely new, start by learning the difference between a public page and a private workspace. Public pages are intended to be viewed by visitors, such as published community articles, creator portfolios and public movie listings. Private workspaces are associated with your signed-in account and are intended for your own information, such as personal notes, saved files, watchlists and account settings. Before entering information, check which account is signed in, whether the page says that an item is private or public, and whether a save, publish or share action will make the information visible to other people. You can explore the main site first and create an account when you reach a feature that needs one.

2. Accounts: registration, sign-in and account types

To register, open the ConfigKit sign-in page, choose the sign-up option and provide the requested information using an email address that you control. Follow the confirmation instructions sent to that inbox before relying on the account for private or community features; if the message does not arrive, check junk or spam and use the resend option on the sign-in page when available. Once your email is confirmed, return to ConfigKit and sign in. The site may present the Privacy Policy and Terms for review, ask optional questions about how you plan to use the service, or offer an optional account-security setup flow. Optional onboarding questions can be skipped if you are not ready to answer them, and you can explore the account settings later. Never create an account using an email address that you cannot access, because email access may be needed for confirmation, sign-in links and account recovery.

ConfigKit account types or profile labels—such as Personal, Developer, Education, Work or Organization—help describe the way you intend to use the site and can tailor the dashboard or profile fields. Selecting an account type does not independently verify that you are employed by an organisation, enrolled in a school, a teacher, a student or a legal representative, and it does not automatically grant administrator privileges or managed-device access. Family, education, group and organisation features may require a separate invitation, approval, membership or relationship configured in the relevant workspace. Choose the profile type that best matches your intended use, provide only information that is appropriate to share, and do not treat a profile label as proof of identity or authority.

For returning members, use the sign-in method configured for your account on the login page. Depending on the options enabled for the account, this may include an email and password, an email magic link, a connected identity provider or a passkey. A magic link is a one-time sign-in link sent to an existing account's email address; it is not the same as registering a new account. A passkey uses the device, password manager, biometric prompt, screen lock or security key associated with its registration. If you cannot sign in, first check that you are using the correct email address and sign-in method, that your email account is accessible, and that the link or code has not expired. Do not send passwords, verification codes or recovery information to anyone claiming to be support.

Open Account Settings to review the profile and account controls currently available to you. Depending on the options shown, you may be able to update your display name or profile details, request an email change, change your password, review sign-in providers, manage security factors or inspect active sessions. Email changes may require confirmation through messages sent to the current or new address, so do not assume the change is complete until the site confirms it. Before disconnecting a connected sign-in provider, make sure another working sign-in method is already linked to the account. For detailed step-by-step instructions about registration, magic links, passkeys, two-factor authentication, recovery codes and sessions, read the dedicated Account Guide.

3. Your dashboard and finding features

After signing in, open the main dashboard to find account shortcuts and the tools available to your profile. The dashboard is a starting point rather than a separate copy of every workspace, so each feature generally has its own page and its own instructions. Use the Official Wiki when you are unsure what a page does, the Universal Search when you want to locate site content or tools, and the Activity Centre or Notifications to review updates that the relevant feature sends to your account. Some dashboards are designed for a particular role or relationship: the Family Dashboard, Education Dashboard, Student Dashboard, Developer Dashboard, Enterprise Dashboard and Organisation Dashboard do not all show the same controls, and access depends on the account relationships and permissions configured for that workspace.

ConfigKit includes personal, developer, family, education and organisation-oriented areas, but a page being visible in the site directory does not mean every visitor can use every action on it. A visitor may be able to read public information while a signed-in member can create or manage their own content, and a particular family or school action may be limited to approved members. If a button is unavailable, a list is empty or a page asks you to sign in, check the message on that page and confirm that the relevant account relationship has been set up rather than assuming that the feature is broken.

4. Configuration profiles and device tools

Mobileconfig Studio helps you create and review supported Apple configuration-profile payloads, including Wi-Fi, Web Clip and encrypted DNS options where available. A configuration profile is a settings file that can change how a device connects to networks, opens web links, configures supported services or applies other settings, so it should be treated as a powerful configuration document rather than an ordinary picture or text file. Use the builder to enter the settings you intend to configure, inspect the generated XML and profile details, and download the resulting .mobileconfig file only after checking that the content is correct. Signed-in features may let you save generated profiles in a private Profile Library. Other supporting tools include the Profile Library for saved work, Profile Viewer for inspecting profile contents, Prepare Profile for preparing or validating a profile file, the DNS Builder for supported DNS configuration, and the Web Clip Builder for Home Screen web shortcuts.

Before installing any configuration profile on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, read its contents and make sure you understand who created it and what each payload does. Profiles can affect network settings, certificates, device restrictions and other system behaviour; a successful XML or property-list validation only checks structure and is not a guarantee that the profile is safe, trustworthy or compatible with your device. Never install a profile just because a website says it is required, and never upload Wi-Fi passwords, private keys, certificates, access tokens or other secrets to a public post or shared profile. For device-specific installation instructions and security considerations, use the Profile Safety guidance and the platform documentation from the device manufacturer.

5. Files, documents and personal productivity

ConfigKit Drive is the browser-based file and folder workspace for storing and organising private files, while Document Vault provides another focused area for important personal documents. The exact file-size limits, preview formats and sharing controls are shown by the relevant page; where a temporary share link is offered, read its expiry and access description before sending it to someone else. A shared link should be treated like a key to the shared item for as long as it remains valid, so avoid sharing sensitive material with people who do not need it and revoke or stop using a link when access is no longer appropriate. ConfigKit Drive is not automatically a synchronised local hard drive, and you should keep separate backups of important files.

For everyday organisation, use the Daily Planner to record tasks and upcoming events, Notes & Ideas to capture information, and Habit Tracker to record recurring routines and review consistency. The Money Centre is intended for manually entered money records, accounts, bills and activity; it should not be mistaken for a bank connection or a service that transfers money. Personal Insights, ConfigKit Insights and Advanced Analytics provide summaries or reporting for supported information already recorded in ConfigKit. Where CSV export is offered, check the exported file before sharing it because it may contain personal or project information.

6. Creative tools, projects and ConfigKit Movies

ConfigKit includes creative and project workspaces for different kinds of media and making. Scratch Project Studio can load and run supported Scratch projects and offers a signed-in workflow for saving project copies to Drive. Video Studio and Photo Gallery provide media-oriented pages, while Projects and Tool Studio help organise or explore project work. The precise editing, import and export options are the ones shown in each tool; do not assume that a page supports professional multi-track editing or every media format unless its controls explicitly say so. For shared documents and presentations, Collaboration Studio supports live co-editing and related collaboration workflows for members who have access to the shared item.

ConfigKit Movies is a movie discovery and creator-publishing workspace within the website, not a separate movie-streaming subscription or an automatic film-production service. Members can create private or public movie listings, add a title, description, genre, release year, poster and an external trailer or video URL, and publish a public movie page when ready. Public movie pages can support ratings and reviews, creator replies and creator-follow actions; signed-in members can manage private watchlists and collections, while genre-based recommendations use activity such as saved titles and highly rated genres rather than claiming to be an AI critic. Creators can review available analytics for their published films, and followers may receive notifications when a creator publishes. The first published listing on ConfigKit Movies is Big Buck Bunny (2008). When adding a movie, use a video URL that you have permission to share, check that the video is actually playable for your intended audience, credit the original creators where required, and follow the source video's licence and platform rules.

Community and creator publishing tools also include the Template Marketplace for reusable templates, Creator Profiles for public portfolios, and the community publishing spaces described below. A public listing or portfolio may be visible to people who are not signed in, so review its title, description, images, links and attachments before publishing. Only upload material you created yourself or have permission to distribute.

7. Community, groups and publishing

The Community area helps members discover profiles and participate in community activity. Depending on the page and the permissions available to your account, members may follow profiles, view shared contributions, comment or publish supported content. Groups allow members to create or join a group using the available invitation process, view membership and collaborate within the group's intended scope. Builder Discussion provides a place for supported discussions, comments and shared profile-related posts, while the Community Store provides community listings and related history. Before posting or joining, read the visible group or community rules and make sure that you are comfortable with the information that will become visible to other members.

The Community Wiki is for member-written knowledge articles: signed-in members can create and manage their own contributions, while published visible articles can be read publicly and reported for review. The Community Book is a separate reading and publishing experience, and writing access follows its author-approval process rather than being automatically granted to every account. The Creator Profiles directory helps visitors discover public portfolios and published work. Use original writing and images, cite sources where appropriate, respect copyright and licences, and do not publish personal information, credentials, harassment, unsafe instructions or content that you do not have permission to distribute.

8. Family, children and education workspaces

ConfigKit provides family and education-oriented areas, including the Family Dashboard, Family Planner, Kids Zone, Kids Messages, the Education Dashboard and the Student Dashboard. These tools are designed for different roles and relationships, so the options available to a parent, child, teacher or student can differ. Family-related features may require a Family ID or an approved connection, and education features may require a configured school, teacher or student relationship; selecting an Education account type by itself does not create those relationships. Kids Messages is designed around parent-supervised communication with approved contacts and the controls described in its adult or family area. Adults should review the available supervision, contact and privacy settings before inviting a child to use a feature, and children should use the service with the guidance of their parent, guardian or school.

Education workspaces can include teaching resources, lessons, student records, assignments and learning activities depending on the signed-in role and the setup completed by the responsible adult or organisation. The Student Dashboard is intended for the student-facing view of assigned work and available learning resources, while the Planner may show supported education items as read-only entries. Treat student and family information as sensitive, share it only with approved people, and follow the policies of the family or educational organisation using the workspace.

9. Privacy, security and responsible use

ConfigKit uses account authentication and access controls for features that store private information or support collaboration. The information handled by a feature depends on what you choose to enter, upload, save, share or publish, and on the account permissions and backend configuration that apply to that feature. Use a unique password, keep your email account secure, enable an available second factor or passkey if it suits your situation, and store recovery codes somewhere private and accessible to you. Never share your password, one-time verification codes, authenticator setup keys, passkeys or recovery codes with another person. Review connected sign-in providers and active sessions from Account Settings when those controls are available, and sign out on shared devices.

Think carefully before making an item public. Public movie listings, creator pages, community posts, published Wiki articles, public templates and shared links may be visible outside your immediate group. Private records should remain in private workspaces, but you should still review the page's access description and avoid uploading information that does not belong there. Read the Privacy Policy, Terms of Use and Safety guidance for the current explanations of the service. ConfigKit is an independent project and is not an Apple service, a bank, a school identity provider or a replacement for a professionally managed device-management system.

10. Downloads, devices and installation

The Downloads page is the starting point for platform packages and installation instructions. Choose only the package intended for your operating system and device architecture, read the release information and verify that the file comes from the official project release location before installing it. The iPhone and iPad workflow is different from a conventional app installation: supported configuration-profile tasks are performed through the browser-based Mobileconfig Studio, which can generate a personalised .mobileconfig file for you to review and install using Apple's profile settings flow. Downloading a desktop wrapper or mobile package does not mean that every web workspace becomes a fully offline application; many features still rely on an internet connection and the hosted ConfigKit services. If a platform warns that a package is unsigned or not notarised, pause and make an informed decision rather than bypassing a security warning automatically.

11. Troubleshooting and getting help

If a page is blank, appears out of date or does not respond, refresh it, try a current browser, check your internet connection and confirm that JavaScript and required site permissions are available. If an account feature does not load, sign out and back in, verify that you are using the correct account, and check whether the page requires a particular family, education, group or creator relationship. If an upload or download fails, check the file type and size, available device storage, browser download permissions and the instructions shown by that page. If a confirmation email or magic link is missing, check spam or junk and request a new link through the official sign-in page. Do not repeatedly submit sensitive forms or share screenshots that reveal passwords, tokens, private documents or recovery codes.

For feature-specific instructions, search the Official Wiki and browse its topic sections; for account registration, sign-in and security, use the dedicated Account Guide; for device profile risks, read Profile Safety; and for installation help, use the Downloads page and its platform guide. If you still need assistance, submit a request through ConfigKit Support and describe the page, the action you attempted, what you expected to happen and the non-sensitive error message you received. Never include your password, one-time code, recovery code, private key or other authentication secret in a support request.

Documentation note: ConfigKit changes over time. This guide describes the main areas and intended workflows, but individual feature availability can depend on sign-in status, account permissions, family or education relationships, service configuration and the controls currently displayed on the page. When a feature's on-screen instructions differ from this guide, follow the current on-screen instructions and report unclear or outdated documentation through Support.
